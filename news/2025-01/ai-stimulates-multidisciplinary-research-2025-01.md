## AI Stimulates Multidisciplinary Research Approaches

**Source:** Inside Higher Ed  
**Date:** January 22, 2025  
**URL:** [https://www.insidehighered.com/opinion/blogs/online-trending-now/2025/01/22/rise-multidisciplinary-research-stimulated-ai](https://www.insidehighered.com/opinion/blogs/online-trending-now/2025/01/22/rise-multidisciplinary-research-stimulated-ai)  
**Summary:** AI tools like Stanford Storm are enabling the synthesis of multi-source information into comprehensive texts, fostering interdisciplinary research and uncovering new knowledge by blending perspectives from diverse fields.

---

### What Happened

The article discusses the transformative impact of AI research tools on academic and scholarly research, emphasizing their role in promoting multidisciplinary collaboration. It highlights the development of Storm, an AI tool by Stanford's Open Virtual Assistant Lab (OVAL), which automates the creation of structured, cited articles resembling Wikipedia entries. This tool utilizes large language models (LLMs) and web-based information retrieval to assist researchers, students, and content creators in knowledge curation and report generation.

### Why It Matters

The integration of AI into research processes is reshaping traditional academic methodologies. By facilitating the synthesis of information across various disciplines, AI tools like Storm enable researchers to uncover new insights and foster interdisciplinary collaboration. This approach not only accelerates the research process but also broadens the scope of inquiry, leading to more comprehensive and innovative outcomes.

### Who's Involved

- **Stanford University's Open Virtual Assistant Lab (OVAL):** Developed Storm, an AI tool designed to automate the creation of structured, cited articles.

- **Ray Schroeder:** The author discussing the impact of AI tools on multidisciplinary research.

### Technical Details

- **Storm:** An open-source AI research tool developed by Stanford's OVAL, launched in early 2024. It automates the creation of structured, cited articles resembling Wikipedia entries by integrating LLMs, such as OpenAI’s GPT-4, with search engines like Bing or Tavily to collect and synthesize online information. Outputs are formatted like Wikipedia articles, with a title, introduction, sections, subheadings, conclusion, and references. Users can review the tool’s research process, termed "BrainSTORMing," which documents the AI’s information-gathering and question-asking steps.

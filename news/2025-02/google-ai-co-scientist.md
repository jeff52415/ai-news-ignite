## Google Develops AI Co-Scientist to Aid Researchers

**Source:** Reuters  
**Date:** February 19, 2025  
**URL:** [https://www.reuters.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/google-develops-ai-co-scientist-aid-researchers-2025-02-19/](https://www.reuters.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/google-develops-ai-co-scientist-aid-researchers-2025-02-19/)  
**Summary:** Google introduces an AI tool designed to assist biomedical researchers by processing extensive literature and generating new hypotheses, aiming to accelerate scientific discovery.

---

### What Happened

Google has developed an AI tool to act as a virtual collaborator for biomedical scientists. The tool, tested by scientists at Stanford University and Imperial College London, uses advanced reasoning to help scientists synthesize vast amounts of literature and generate novel hypotheses. ([reuters.com](https://www.reuters.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/google-develops-ai-co-scientist-aid-researchers-2025-02-19/?utm_source=openai))

### Why It Matters

This development signifies a significant advancement in integrating AI into scientific research, potentially accelerating the pace of biomedical discoveries and enhancing the efficiency of researchers.

### Who's Involved

- **Google:** Developed the AI co-scientist tool.
- **DeepMind:** Google's AI division that has prioritized scientific applications.
- **Demis Hassabis:** CEO of DeepMind, co-recipient of a Nobel Prize in Chemistry for technology developed in the AI unit.
- **Stanford University and Imperial College London:** Institutions that tested the AI tool.
- **Vivek Natarajan:** Google scientist who stated the tool would complement rather than replace researchers.

### Technical Details

- **AI Co-Scientist:** A multi-agent AI system built with Gemini 2.0, designed to assist scientists in generating novel hypotheses and research proposals. ([research.google](https://research.google/blog/accelerating-scientific-breakthroughs-with-an-ai-co-scientist/?utm_source=openai))
- **Testing:** The tool has been tested in experiments on liver fibrosis, where it suggested approaches that showed promising activity and potential to inhibit causes of disease. ([reuters.com](https://www.reuters.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/google-develops-ai-co-scientist-aid-researchers-2025-02-19/?utm_source=openai))

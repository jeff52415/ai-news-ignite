## 📰 Scientists use quantum machine learning to create semiconductors for the first time – and it could transform how chips are made

**Source:** Live Science  
**Date:** 2025-06-23  
**URL:** [https://www.livescience.com/technology/computing/scientists-use-quantum-machine-learning-to-create-semiconductors-for-the-first-time-and-it-could-transform-how-chips-are-made](https://www.livescience.com/technology/computing/scientists-use-quantum-machine-learning-to-create-semiconductors-for-the-first-time-and-it-could-transform-how-chips-are-made)  
**Summary:** Researchers in Australia have developed a new quantum machine learning model, QKAR, to predict properties in semiconductor manufacturing with greater accuracy, potentially transforming chip production by improving modeling of complex nonlinear phenomena.

---

### 🔹 What Happened

Australian researchers at the Commonwealth Scientific and Industrial Research Organisation (CSIRO) have developed a novel quantum machine learning technique called Quantum Kernel-Aligned Regressor (QKAR). This method enhances the prediction of Ohmic contact resistance in semiconductor fabrication, a critical factor affecting chip performance. By converting classical data into quantum states and analyzing it with machine learning, QKAR has demonstrated up to 20% greater efficiency compared to traditional models.  ([livescience.com](https://www.livescience.com/technology/computing/scientists-use-quantum-machine-learning-to-create-semiconductors-for-the-first-time-and-it-could-transform-how-chips-are-made?utm_source=openai))

### 🔹 Why It Matters

The semiconductor manufacturing process is intricate and demands high precision. Accurately modeling Ohmic contact resistance is essential for optimizing chip performance. Traditional machine learning approaches often struggle with small datasets and nonlinear behaviors. QKAR's ability to effectively handle high-dimensional, small-sample regression tasks could revolutionize chip design and production, leading to more efficient and powerful electronic devices.  ([livescience.com](https://www.livescience.com/technology/computing/scientists-use-quantum-machine-learning-to-create-semiconductors-for-the-first-time-and-it-could-transform-how-chips-are-made?utm_source=openai))

### 🔹 Who's Involved

- **CSIRO (Commonwealth Scientific and Industrial Research Organisation):** Australia's national research agency, leading the development of QKAR.
- **Professor Muhammad Usman:** Head of Quantum Systems at CSIRO and senior author of the study.

### 🔹 Technical Details

- **Model:** Quantum Kernel-Aligned Regressor (QKAR)
- **Methodology:** Converts classical data into quantum states using a Pauli-Z quantum feature map, followed by a quantum kernel alignment layer for machine learning analysis.
- **Data:** 159 experimental samples of gallium nitride high-electron-mobility transistors (GaN HEMTs)
- **Benchmark Results:** QKAR achieved a mean absolute error (MAE) of 0.338 Ω·mm, outperforming seven classical machine learning models.  ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10803))

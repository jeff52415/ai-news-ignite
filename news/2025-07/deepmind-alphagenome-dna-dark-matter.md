## DeepMind's AlphaGenome Maps DNA's 'Dark Matter'

**Summary:** DeepMind's new AI system, AlphaGenome, analyzes non-coding DNA to predict gene regulation patterns, advancing genomic medicine and drug discovery.

---

### What Happened

DeepMind introduced AlphaGenome, an AI model designed to predict how single-nucleotide variants in non-coding DNA sequences affect gene regulation. This model processes DNA sequences up to one million base pairs, providing high-resolution predictions across various molecular properties. AlphaGenome is available via API for non-commercial research. 

### Why It Matters

AlphaGenome addresses the challenge of understanding the vast non-coding regions of the genome, which constitute 98% of human DNA and play a crucial role in gene regulation. By accurately predicting the impact of genetic variants in these regions, AlphaGenome has the potential to revolutionize our understanding of disease mechanisms and accelerate the development of targeted therapies. 

### Who's Involved

- **DeepMind**: Developed AlphaGenome to advance genomic research and medicine.
- **Scientific Community**: Utilizing AlphaGenome for non-commercial research to explore gene regulation and disease mechanisms.

### Technical Details

- **Model**: AlphaGenome processes DNA sequences up to one million base pairs, predicting thousands of molecular properties, including gene expression levels, chromatin accessibility, and RNA splicing patterns. 
- **Architecture**: Combines convolutional layers to detect local patterns, transformers to model long-range dependencies, and modality-specific layers for final predictions. 
- **Training Data**: Utilizes large public datasets from consortia like ENCODE, GTEx, 4D Nucleome, and FANTOM5, covering hundreds of human and mouse cell types and tissues.

## 📰 EU Releases Comprehensive AI Regulatory Framework

**Source:** TS2 Tech  
**Date:** 2025-07-20

**Summary:** The European Union has introduced detailed regulatory guidelines requiring advanced AI systems to undergo safety tests, transparency reporting, and cybersecurity measures under the AI Act.

---

### 🔹 What Happened

The European Union unveiled the AI Act, a comprehensive regulatory framework for artificial intelligence. This new law requires advanced AI systems to pass safety and risk assessments, transparency obligations, and implement strong cybersecurity protocols. AI applications are classified by risk levels—unacceptable, high, limited, and minimal—with specific rules applicable to each category. Prohibited AI practices include subliminal manipulation and real-time biometric surveillance in public spaces. The Act aims to ensure that AI technologies are safe, transparent, and trustworthy. 

### 🔹 Why It Matters

This legislation sets a new global standard for AI regulation, seeking to harmonize the European AI market while protecting fundamental rights and public safety. It fosters responsible AI innovation by imposing clear rules, ensuring that AI benefits society without undue risks. The phased rollout gives stakeholders time to comply, with full enforcement by August 2026.

### 🔹 Who's Involved

- **European Commission:** Developed and proposed the AI Act.
- **European Parliament and Council:** Passed the Act into law.
- **European Artificial Intelligence Board (EAIB):** Coordinates enforcement among member states.
- **National Competent Authorities:** Oversee implementation and compliance within countries.

### 🔹 Technical Details

- **Risk Levels for AI Systems:**
  - **Unacceptable Risk:** AI uses banned outright (e.g., subliminal techniques, live biometric surveillance).
  - **High Risk:** AI systems with critical impact are subject to strict risk management, transparency, and cybersecurity requirements.
  - **Limited Risk:** Systems requiring transparency measures (e.g., chatbots identifying themselves).
  - **Minimal Risk:** Generally exempt and no specific regulation applied.
- **General-Purpose AI Obligations:** Providers must evaluate risks, provide transparency about capabilities and limitations, comply with copyright protections, and implement additional safeguards for systemic risks, including incident reporting and cybersecurity measures.

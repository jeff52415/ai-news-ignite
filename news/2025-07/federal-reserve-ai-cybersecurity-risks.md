### 🔹 What Happened

The Federal Reserve's annual cybersecurity report has identified artificial intelligence (AI), especially generative AI and quantum computing, as emerging threats to the financial system. The report emphasizes the risk of AI-driven cyber-attacks and underscores the necessity for collaborative security measures between the government and the private sector.

### 🔹 Why It Matters

As AI technologies advance, they present new challenges to cybersecurity, particularly in the financial sector. The potential for AI to automate cyber-attacks increases the complexity and scale of threats, making it imperative for both public and private entities to work together to enhance the resilience of financial systems.

### 🔹 Who's Involved

- **Federal Reserve:** As the central banking system of the United States, the Federal Reserve is responsible for overseeing the stability of the financial system and has highlighted AI and cybersecurity risks in its annual report.

- **White & Case:** A global law firm that has analyzed and reported on the Federal Reserve's findings, providing insights into the implications of AI and cybersecurity risks in the financial sector.

### 🔹 Technical Details

- **Generative AI:** A subset of AI that can generate new content, such as text, images, or videos, which can be exploited for malicious purposes like creating deepfakes or automating phishing attacks.

- **Quantum Computing:** An emerging field of computing that leverages quantum mechanics to process information in fundamentally different ways, potentially rendering current encryption methods obsolete and posing new challenges to data security.

- **Cyber-Automated Attacks:** Cyber-attacks that are automated using AI technologies, allowing for faster and more sophisticated attacks that can adapt to and bypass traditional security measures.

The report does not provide specific benchmark results but highlights the increasing sophistication of cyber threats enabled by AI and quantum computing.

## 📰 Study Reveals AI-Driven Changes in Over One Million Scientific Papers

**Source:** ModernSciences.org  
**Date:** August 22, 2025  
**URL:** [https://modernsciences.org/ai-in-science-academic-writing-august-2025/](https://modernsciences.org/ai-in-science-academic-writing-august-2025/)  
**Summary:** An analysis of over 1.1 million scientific publications showed a rise in AI-modified content, particularly in abstracts and introductions, with computer science papers showing up to 22% LLM modification. This trend reflects AI’s growing influence in academic scientific writing.

---

### 🔹 What Happened

A comprehensive study analyzed more than 1.1 million scientific papers published between January 2020 and September 2024. The research found a steady increase in the use of large language models (LLMs) for academic writing, with computer science papers exhibiting up to 22% LLM modification. The most common sections affected were abstracts and introductions. ([modernsciences.org](https://modernsciences.org/ai-in-science-academic-writing-august-2025/?utm_source=openai))

### 🔹 Why It Matters

The growing integration of AI in academic writing signifies a transformative shift in how research is conducted and communicated. While AI tools can enhance efficiency and assist in drafting, their widespread use raises questions about authorship, originality, and the potential for homogenization of scientific language. ([techradar.com](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/ai-is-redefining-university-research-heres-how?utm_source=openai))

### 🔹 Who's Involved

- **Researchers and Academics:** Utilizing AI tools to streamline the writing process.
- **AI Developers:** Creating and refining LLMs and other AI writing assistants.
- **Academic Publishers:** Adapting to the rise of AI-generated content and considering its implications for peer review and publication standards.

### 🔹 Technical Details

- **Models and Methods:** The study focused on large language models (LLMs) and their application in academic writing.
- **Data:** Analysis covered preprints and published papers from January 2020 to September 2024, sourced from arXiv, bioRxiv, and the Nature portfolio of journals.
- **Findings:** A clear and steady increase in LLM-assisted writing was observed, with computer science leading the trend. ([modernsciences.org](https://modernsciences.org/ai-in-science-academic-writing-august-2025/?utm_source=openai))

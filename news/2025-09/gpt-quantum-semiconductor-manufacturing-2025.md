## 📰 Scientists Apply Quantum Machine Learning to Semiconductor Manufacturing

**Source:** LiveScience  
**Date:** September 29, 2025  
**URL:** [https://www.livescience.com/technology/computing/scientists-use-quantum-machine-learning-to-create-semiconductors-for-the-first-time-and-it-could-transform-how-chips-are-made](https://www.livescience.com/technology/computing/scientists-use-quantum-machine-learning-to-create-semiconductors-for-the-first-time-and-it-could-transform-how-chips-are-made)  
**Summary:** Researchers in Australia have applied quantum machine learning to model Ohmic contact resistance in semiconductors, potentially revolutionizing chip manufacturing with improved precision and efficiency.

---

### 🔹 What Happened

Australian researchers have successfully applied quantum machine learning (QML) techniques to semiconductor manufacturing, marking a significant advancement in chip production. They developed a Quantum Kernel-Aligned Regressor (QKAR) to model Ohmic contact resistance in gallium nitride high-electron-mobility transistors (GaN HEMTs). This approach outperformed traditional machine learning models, demonstrating the potential of QML in enhancing semiconductor fabrication processes.

### 🔹 Why It Matters

The integration of QML into semiconductor manufacturing addresses challenges in modeling complex, nonlinear data, leading to more accurate predictions and optimized fabrication processes. This innovation could streamline chip design and production, resulting in faster development cycles and more efficient devices. As quantum hardware continues to mature, such applications may become increasingly practical, offering a competitive edge in the semiconductor industry.

### 🔹 Who's Involved

- **CSIRO (Commonwealth Scientific and Industrial Research Organisation):** Australia's national science agency, leading the research and development of QML applications in semiconductor manufacturing.

- **University Collaborations:** Academic institutions, including the University of Sydney, contributed to the study, providing expertise in quantum computing and semiconductor physics.

### 🔹 Technical Details

- **Quantum Kernel-Aligned Regressor (QKAR):** A novel QML model designed to handle high-dimensional, small-sample datasets, effectively modeling Ohmic contact resistance in GaN HEMTs.

- **Data Utilized:** 159 experimental samples of GaN HEMTs were used to train the QKAR, demonstrating its capability to extract deep patterns from limited datasets.

- **Performance Metrics:** The QKAR achieved a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 0.338 97mm, outperforming seven classical machine learning models in accuracy and generalization.

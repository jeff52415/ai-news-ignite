## Concerns Over AI-Generated Content in Academic Publishing

**Source:** Times Higher Education  
**Date:** October 16, 2025  
**URL:** [https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/low-quality-and-ai-generated-papers-could-overwhelm-publishing](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/low-quality-and-ai-generated-papers-could-overwhelm-publishing)

**Summary:**

A recent survey of over 3,000 researchers reveals significant concerns about the impact of AI-generated content on academic publishing. The study highlights fears that a surge in low-quality or AI-generated papers could overwhelm peer review processes and compromise research integrity.

---

### What Happened

Cambridge University Press released the "Publishing Futures" report on October 16, 2025, based on a survey of more than 3,000 researchers worldwide. The findings indicate that 53% of respondents believe AI will negatively affect the research publishing system, while 18% feel it will improve academic publishing. Concerns include the proliferation of low-quality AI-generated content and its potential to overwhelm peer review processes. ([timeshighereducation.com](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/low-quality-and-ai-generated-papers-could-overwhelm-publishing?utm_source=openai))

### Why It Matters

The rise of AI-generated content poses challenges to maintaining the quality and credibility of academic publishing. If not addressed, it could lead to an influx of subpar research, making it difficult for peer reviewers to assess meaningful studies and potentially undermining the integrity of the scientific record.

### Who's Involved

- **Cambridge University Press**: Conducted the survey and published the "Publishing Futures" report.
- **Mandy Hill**: Managing Director of Cambridge University Press, who expressed concerns about the impact of AI-assisted papers on the publishing ecosystem. ([timeshighereducation.com](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/low-quality-and-ai-generated-papers-could-overwhelm-publishing?utm_source=openai))

### Technical Details

- **Survey Methodology**: The "Publishing Futures" report is based on a survey of over 3,000 researchers globally.
- **Key Findings**:
  - 53% of respondents believe AI will negatively impact the research publishing system.
  - 18% feel AI will improve academic publishing.
  - 33% are concerned that AI will make it harder for researchers to gain due credit for their work.
  - 81% agree that the increase in research outputs has put the peer review system under pressure. ([timeshighereducation.com](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/low-quality-and-ai-generated-papers-could-overwhelm-publishing?utm_source=openai))
